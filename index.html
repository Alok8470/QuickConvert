Image Compression

WebP vs JPEG vs PNG — Which Format Should You Use in 2026?

A technical comparison of the three most common web image formats. WebP offers 25–35% smaller file sizes than JPEG at equivalent visual quality, with full alpha channel transparency support. JPEG remains the most universally compatible format for photographic content. PNG is the only option for strictly lossless images requiring precise pixel accuracy.

For most modern websites, switching from PNG screenshots to WebP can reduce page weight by 60–80%, directly improving Google Core Web Vitals (LCP and FID scores), which are ranking signals for organic search position. QuickConvert's Smart Auto mode automatically selects the optimal format based on your source image type.

PDF Workflows

How to Compile Scanned Documents into a Professional PDF Archive

Scanning physical documents and photographs creates large image files (often 2–10 MB per page) that are difficult to email, store, or share. By converting your scanned JPG or PNG files into a single multi-page PDF, you create a compact, universally readable archive document.

Best practice: scan at 150–200 DPI for digital-only viewing (keeps file small), or 300 DPI for documents that may also be printed. Use QuickConvert's A4 Portrait layout with 10 mm margins for clean, professional multi-page archival PDFs.

Privacy & Security

Why You Should Never Upload Sensitive Documents to Cloud Converters

Most free online converters require you to upload files to their servers. Under GDPR and CCPA, any service that processes EU or California residents' documents is a data controller — legally required to sign Data Processing Agreements (DPA) and maintain explicit security certifications.

QuickConvert eliminates this legal and security risk entirely. Because all processing happens inside your browser's isolated JavaScript sandbox, we are architecturally incapable of accessing, reading, or storing your documents — no DPA required, no breach risk.
