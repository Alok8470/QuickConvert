How to Convert Text & Notes to PDF — Step-by-Step Guide

1

Enter a Document Title

Optionally add a title in the Document Header field. This will appear as a styled heading at the top of your PDF, automatically separated from the body text with a horizontal rule — ideal for professional reports or memos.

2

Type or Paste Your Content

Type original content or paste text from any source — emails, meeting notes, articles, code documentation, or research drafts. The live word and character counter updates as you type.

3

Customize Typography

Select your preferred font (Helvetica for clean sans-serif, Times for editorial readability, or Courier for monospace/code), adjust body text size (10–15 pt), set margins, and toggle automatic page numbering.

4

Compile & Download

Click Compile & Download PDF. The jsPDF engine automatically flows text across multiple pages with proper word-wrapping, page breaks, and footer page numbers — no layout adjustments needed from you.

Pro Tip: Click Load Sample Text to instantly load a fully formatted executive project brief — this helps you preview exactly how your typography settings will look in the finished PDF before you paste in your own content.

Text to PDF Converter — Frequently Asked Questions

There is no imposed word count or page limit. jsPDF automatically creates additional pages as your text overflows each page boundary, using the font size, margin settings, and page format you selected. Documents of 50+ pages generate in under two seconds on a modern browser.
The Document Title field generates a formatted heading in bold at the top of the PDF. The body content area handles plain text with automatic word wrapping and multi-page flow. For fully styled documents with HTML/Markdown rich formatting (bold, italic, tables, lists), this feature is on our development roadmap.
Helvetica (Clean Sans) is recommended for modern business documents, presentations, and digital reports. Times New Roman (Classic Editorial) is preferred for academic papers, legal filings, and long-form editorial content. Courier suits technical documentation, code snippets, and typewritten memo aesthetics.
